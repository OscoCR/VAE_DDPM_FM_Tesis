\chapter{Introduction}

Unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) equipped with RGB cameras are now a common tool to count plants in the field. Plant density is one of the main factors that determine yield, and measuring it by hand is impractical over large areas because of the labor it requires \cite{Kitano2019}. Detectors applied to UAV images have been used to count crops such as corn \cite{Kitano2019,Osco2021} and to locate and count pineapple fruits \cite{Shiu2023}.

These detectors need annotated images, and in agriculture such data are hard to obtain. A campaign usually covers a few flights over a single field, with one type of soil and similar illumination, so the images show little variability \cite{Gonzalez2026}, and a detector trained on one domain does not necessarily work on another \cite{Bumbaca2025}. In addition, annotating the images by hand is slow and costly \cite{Bah2018,Cai2022}. A way to enrich such small datasets is to generate synthetic images with a generative model, which has already been used to enrich the training data of plant-related tasks \cite{Cai2022,Muhammad2023,Hirahara2025}.

Latent diffusion models \cite{Rombach2022} generate images at a much lower cost than diffusion in pixel space: an autoencoder compresses each image into a latent space, and the diffusion model works there. On small and homogeneous UAV datasets, however, training these models from scratch can be unstable, and it has been shown that a continuous latent space preserves the structure of a pineapple field where a discrete one fails \cite{Gonzalez2026}. The design of the latent space has been studied mostly on large benchmarks \cite{Yao2025,Skorokhodov2025,Zhong2026}. To the best of our knowledge, how the treatments applied to the training data of the autoencoder and the modifications of its structure affect the quality of the generated images has not been studied in a regime of few, homogeneous, and multimodal data. The closest work we found measured a single treatment, preprocessing, in a small medical cohort \cite{Fallah2026}.

This thesis proposal addresses that gap. It evaluates experimentally how treatments of the training data of a variational autoencoder (VAE) and modifications of its structure change the quality, measured with FID and KID, of aerial pineapple images generated with DDPM and Flow Matching, using a factorial design with several seeds. The work continues the research line of the group on pineapple imagery captured by drones \cite{XieLi2024,Gonzalez2026}. Labeling the generated images and using them to train a counting model are left as future work.

Chapter~III states the problem, the hypothesis, the objectives, the research questions, and the scope. Chapter~IV describes the methodology and the design of the experiments. Chapter~V reports the initial results, and Chapter~VI presents the workplan.
