\chapter{Initial Results}

The experiments reported in this chapter were run between 15 September and 14 October 2026 with a single seed per configuration. Their purpose was not to rank configurations but to establish a reliable baseline, to learn what the measurements can and cannot distinguish, and to decide how the main experiments must be designed. Generation quality was measured with FID and KID on 400 generated images against a fixed reference set; DDPM sampled with 1\,000 steps and Flow Matching with 50 Euler steps. Reconstruction was measured on the 346 test images.

\section{Audit of the Training Pipeline}

Before comparing configurations, the training and generation code inherited from the base repository was audited. Seven problems were found, most of them present since the first commit of the base repository, and all were corrected (Table~\ref{tab:audit}).

The effect of these corrections on the FID is small: with the same VAE, the DDPM went from 155.8 (old recipe, mean decoding) to 160.1 (corrected recipe), and Flow Matching with the best checkpoint from 167.2 to 162.8. What changed is the reliability of the pipeline: a Flow Matching model that had silently collapsed now trains stably.

\section{Reconstruction Quality of the VAE Variants}

Table~\ref{tab:recon} reports the reconstruction of the VAE variants on the test set. Adding the adversarial loss and the variational output layer lowers the pixel fidelity by about 1.7~dB, but the reconstructions look more realistic: the FID and KID of the reconstructed images improve when the VAE receives the depth channel. The effect depends on the modality: with an RGB-only input, the same change did not improve the perceptual metrics.

Among the VAEs with adversarial loss, data augmentation lowered the test MSE by 3 to 5\% with respect to the controls trained for the same number of epochs. Masking the input (blocks of $32\times32$ pixels) lowered the unmasked validation reconstruction in an ordered way: PSNR 25.40~dB without masking, 24.62~dB with 25\% of the input masked, and 23.95~dB with 50\% (SSIM 0.977, 0.971, and 0.967). This is expected for a model trained to fill gaps; its effect on generation has not been tested.

Three observations follow. First, in these runs none of the changes to the VAE (adversarial loss, variational layer, augmentation) produced a difference in FID clearly larger than the variability inside a single run: two checkpoints of the same training differ by up to 9 points. The lowest value (DDPM final, 151.4, with a KID of 0.153) is 2.4\% below the reference, which cannot be distinguished from chance with one seed. Second, adding augmentation did not improve generation: with the DDPM final checkpoint, the configuration with augmentation is 7.7 points worse than the one without it. Third, providing the depth channel as an input of the VAE is the only difference that appears in all three comparisons (DDPM, and Flow Matching with the best and the final checkpoint), with 8 to 14 points of FID in favor of the RGB-D input; it was observed with the original recipe and has not been repeated with the corrected one. Flow Matching did not improve the FID over DDPM, but it generated the 400 images in about 11 minutes against 1 to 3 hours for the 1\,000-step DDPM.

\section{Implications for the Research Proposal}

The initial results lead to four decisions for the experimental design of Chapter~IV:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Replicates are necessary.} With one seed per configuration, the differences observed are of the order of the variability between checkpoints, so no ranking can be claimed. The main experiments use several seeds per condition and an analysis of variance.
  \item \textbf{Absence of effect must be testable.} Most of the changes produced no visible difference. The analysis includes equivalence tests, so that a null result can be reported as an effect smaller than a stated margin and not as an inconclusive one.
  \item \textbf{Reconstruction and generation must be reported separately.} Pixel fidelity, perceptual quality of the reconstructions, and generation quality moved in different directions (research question RQ3).
  \item \textbf{Modality is a first-order factor.} In the comparisons made, the only consistent signal was the depth channel, so it is considered in the design from the start and not as a later extension.
\end{enumerate}

In summary, the experiments carried out so far show that the question is open: the usual improvements to the VAE do not transfer in an obvious way to generation in this regime, and the measurement noise is as large as the effects. The proposed design is built to resolve exactly that.
