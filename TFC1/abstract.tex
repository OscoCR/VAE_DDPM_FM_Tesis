Generative models can produce synthetic images that complement the scarce and costly data of precision agriculture, but their quality depends on a component that is seldom examined when data are few and homogeneous: the latent space of the autoencoder on which latent diffusion models work. Such images could later enrich the datasets of downstream tasks, such as plant counting. This proposal studies how treatments of the training data of a variational autoencoder (augmentation, random masking, selection of images, and the depth modality of the input, RGB or RGB with estimated depth) and modifications of its structure (a variational output layer and an adversarial loss) affect the quality of aerial pineapple images generated with a denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM) and Flow Matching (FM), from a small, homogeneous dataset.

The hypothesis is that these treatments and modifications produce measurable differences in the generation quality, as assessed by the Fr\'echet Inception Distance (FID) and the Kernel Inception Distance (KID), with respect to a base VAE. It is tested with an experimental design in stages: a fractional factorial screening with replicates, a refinement of the factors that matter, and a confirmation with FM, analyzed with analysis of variance and equivalence tests. Preliminary experiments show that the differences between configurations are of the size of the variability between checkpoints of a single run, and that the depth channel is the only consistent signal; this motivates the use of replicates. The expected result is evidence of which treatments and modifications matter, and which do not, for generating aerial agricultural imagery from few data.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} latent diffusion, variational autoencoder, Flow Matching, UAV imagery, small datasets, factorial design.
