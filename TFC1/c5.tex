\chapter{Initial Results}

The experiments reported in this chapter were run between 15 September and 14 October 2026 with a single seed per configuration. Their purpose was not to rank configurations but to establish a reliable baseline, to learn what the measurements can and cannot distinguish, and to decide how the main experiments must be designed. Generation quality was measured with FID and KID on 400 generated images against a fixed reference set made of the 346 test images, a protocol set by the cost of sampling that the main experiments replace with a larger number of images (Chapter~IV); DDPM sampled with 1\,000 steps and Flow Matching (FM) with 50 Euler steps. Reconstruction was measured on the 346 test images.

\section{Audit of the Training Pipeline}

Before comparing configurations, the training and generation code inherited from the base repository was audited. Seven problems were found, most of them present since the first commit of the base repository, and all were corrected; the details are in Annex~\ref{app:audit} (Table~\ref{tab:audit}). Three of them affect the results directly: the learning-rate schedule completed about four cosine cycles instead of one, the best checkpoint was chosen by an arbitrary rule, and the pixel noise of the variational output layer was left on at generation time, which raised the FID of the DDPM generations from 155.8 to 173.4. The effect of all the corrections on the FID is small: with the same VAE, the DDPM went from 155.8 (old recipe, mean decoding) to 160.1 (corrected recipe), and FM with the best checkpoint from 167.2 to 162.8. What changed is the reliability of the pipeline: an FM model that had silently collapsed now trains stably.

\section{Reconstruction Quality of the VAE Variants}

Table~\ref{tab:recon} reports the reconstruction of the VAE variants on the test set. Adding the adversarial loss and the variational output layer lowers the pixel fidelity by about 1.7~dB, and the FID and KID of the reconstructed images improve when the VAE receives the depth channel. Figure~\ref{fig:recon} shows what changes: the reconstructions of the variant with adversarial loss and variational layer have sharper edges and a finer grain than those of the other variants, which are smoother, but the grain also appears where the original is smooth, because the reconstruction shown is the sampled output of the variational layer, which includes pixel noise. The better FID of the reconstructions therefore reflects a texture that resembles that of the real images, and not a more faithful copy of them. The effect depends on the modality: with an RGB-only input, the same change did not improve the perceptual metrics.

Among the VAEs with adversarial loss, data augmentation lowered the test MSE by 3 to 5\% with respect to the controls trained for the same number of epochs. Masking the input (blocks of $32\times32$ pixels) lowered the unmasked validation reconstruction in an ordered way: PSNR 25.40~dB without masking, 24.62~dB with 25\% of the input masked, and 23.95~dB with 50\% (SSIM 0.977, 0.971, and 0.967). This is expected for a model trained to fill gaps; its effect on generation has not been tested.

Three observations follow. First, in these runs the changes to the VAE (adversarial loss, variational layer, augmentation) produced differences in FID of the same size as the variability inside a single run: two checkpoints of the same training differ by up to 9 points, and the differences between the first three rows do not exceed 9.2 points, except for the final FM checkpoints of the first and second rows (13.9 points), which also differ in the recipe. Only the comparison of augmentation against no augmentation uses the same recipe, so the others are indications and not controlled effects. The lowest value (DDPM final, 151.4, with a KID of 0.153) is 2.4\% below the reference, which cannot be distinguished from chance with one seed. Second, adding augmentation did not improve generation: with the DDPM final checkpoint, the configuration with augmentation is 7.7 points worse than the one without it. Third, providing the depth channel as an input of the VAE is the only difference that appears in all three comparisons (DDPM, and FM with the best and the final checkpoint), with 8 to 14 points of FID in favor of the RGB-D input; it was observed with the original recipe and has not been repeated with the corrected one. FM did not improve the FID over DDPM, but it generated the 400 images in about 11 minutes against 1 to 3 hours for the 1\,000-step DDPM. Figure~\ref{fig:samples} shows six real test crops and the first six images generated by each generator for the VAE with adversarial loss, variational layer, and augmentation. The samples reproduce the color and the texture of the leaves, but they have sharp artifacts at the edges of the leaves and areas of flat color, and two of the six FM samples show bare soil. This is consistent with the FID of 159.1 and 170.6 of these generators (Table~\ref{tab:gen}): the generations are recognizable as pineapple fields, but they can be told apart from the real images.

\section{Implications for the Research Proposal}

The initial results lead to four decisions for the experimental design of Chapter~IV:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Replicates are necessary.} With one seed per configuration, the differences observed between configurations are mostly within the range of the variability between checkpoints of a single run (up to 9 points), so no ranking can be claimed; this range is only an indication of the noise, which the main experiments measure directly with replicates. The main experiments will use several seeds per condition (three replicates per cell are planned, Chapter~IV) and will be analyzed with an analysis of variance.
  \item \textbf{Absence of effect must be testable.} Most of the changes produced no visible difference. The analysis will include equivalence tests, so that a null result can be reported as an effect smaller than a stated margin and not as an inconclusive one.
  \item \textbf{Reconstruction and generation must be reported separately.} Pixel fidelity, perceptual quality of the reconstructions, and generation quality moved in different directions (objective 4).
  \item \textbf{Modality is a first-order factor.} In the comparisons made, the only consistent signal was the depth channel, so it is considered in the design from the start and not as a later extension.
\end{enumerate}

In summary, the experiments carried out so far show that the question is open: the usual improvements to the VAE do not transfer in an obvious way to generation in this regime, and, except for the depth channel, the differences between configurations are mostly within the range of the variability between checkpoints of a single run. The proposed design is built to resolve exactly that.
